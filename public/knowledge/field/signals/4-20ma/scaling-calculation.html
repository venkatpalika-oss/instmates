4–20 mA Scaling & Calculation

Scaling converts the 4–20 mA signal into meaningful engineering units such as bar, °C, %, ppm, or flow rate. Every PLC, DCS, and analyzer system depends on correct scaling.

Basic Concept

  • 4 mA = Lower Range Value (LRV)
  • 20 mA = Upper Range Value (URV)
  • Span = URV − LRV
  • Total mA span = 16 mA

mA → Engineering Units (Forward Calculation)

Process Value =
(Current − 4) ÷ 16 × Span + LRV

Use this formula when the current is linear and direct-acting with respect to the displayed quantity.

Worked Example 1 (Simple Range)

Pressure transmitter range: 0–100 bar

  • LRV = 0
  • URV = 100
  • Span = 100

If measured current = 12 mA: 

(12 − 4) ÷ 16 × 100
= 8 ÷ 16 × 100
= 50 bar

Worked Example 2 (Negative Range)

DP transmitter range: -50 to +50 mbar

  • LRV = -50
  • URV = +50
  • Span = 100

If current = 12 mA: 

(12 − 4) ÷ 16 × 100 + (-50)
= 50 − 50
= 0 mbar

Negative ranges are common in DP level and flow applications.

Engineering Units → mA (Reverse Calculation)

mA =
((Process Value − LRV) ÷ Span) × 16 + 4

Example

Flow range: 0–200 m³/h Actual flow = 100 m³/h 

((100 − 0) ÷ 200) × 16 + 4
= 0.5 × 16 + 4
= 12 mA

Quick Percentage Method

If signal is at 50% of span:

  • 50% of 16 mA = 8 mA
  • 8 + 4 = 12 mA

Useful for quick field estimations without full calculation.

Predict the current

Before opening the lab, predict the current at each process value:

  • Pressure: 7.5 bar on a 0 to 10 bar transmitter.
  • Temperature: −10 °C on a −20 to +20 °C transmitter.
Show worked answers

Pressure: 16 mA. At 7.5 bar the transmitter is at 75% of span. Add 75% of 16 mA to the 4 mA baseline: 4 + 12 = 16 mA.

Temperature: 8 mA. Start at −20 °C. A rise of 10 °C covers one quarter of the 40 °C span, so the current is 4 + 4 = 8 mA. Check in reverse: −20 + (8 − 4) ÷ 16 × 40 = −10 °C. A negative temperature can give a normal loop current.

Diagnose a DCS scaling mismatch

Simulated exercise: the pressure is 7.5 bar and the receiver sees 16 mA. How could the DCS display 3.75 bar?

Test your prediction in LAB 01 (simulator requires JavaScript). Use Back to return to the guide.

  1. Select Reset workbench. Keep Follow process value, fault Normal and the 0 to 10 bar transmitter range. Set Process value to 7.5. Compare the ideal output, loop current, DCS input and engineering indication.
  2. Under PLC / DCS scale, leave Value at 4 mA at 0. Change only Value at 20 mA from 10 to 5. Which reading changes? Does the receiver flag the current as bad?
  3. Select Match transmitter range without changing the pressure. Compare the same readings again.
Check the three readings

The indication goes from 7.5 bar to 3.75 bar and back to 7.5 bar. Ideal output, loop current and DCS input stay at 16 mA throughout. Both receiver scales interpret 16 mA as 75%: on the incorrect scale, 75% of 5 bar is 3.75 bar. Current quality stays GOOD because the current is in range; the receiver cannot tell from current alone which engineering endpoints you intended.

Verify the result and its limits

Explain why matching the range restored the indication without changing the current. Then try the temperature prediction: select °C, set LRV to −20, URV to +20 and process value to −10. Select Match transmitter range. Expect 8 mA and −10 °C. Changing the unit label alone does not convert the numbers.

LAB 01 assumes linear, direct-acting, steady-state operation. It does not model wiring resistance, supply compliance, square-root extraction or process dynamics. Agreement here does not verify a real instrument’s calibration, measure its current or diagnose a field fault. All changes in this exercise are on the virtual bench.

Common Field Errors

  • Wrong LRV / URV in PLC
  • Units mismatch (bar vs kPa)
  • 4–20 reversed (20–4 configuration)
  • Scaling applied twice (transmitter + DCS)
  • Incorrect square root extraction for DP flow

Golden Field Rule

Always confirm transmitter range from nameplate or configuration before adjusting PLC/DCS scaling.
